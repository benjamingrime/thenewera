Case Studies: The 2026 Frontier

So we've got a machine that can ace every standardised test we throw at it. So what? What does that look like in the real world? The following are all from models like ChatGPT.

1. Mathematics: The Erdos Conquest

Paul Erdős posed 1,000 mathematical problems informally over 50 years -- only catalogued as a formal list in 2023. The number solved rose from ~350 to ~480 in six months, driven by LLM-assisted proof search. Results verified by Terence Tao, the Messi of Maths.

2. Physics: Solving a Decades-Old Problem in a Day

A Brookhaven physicist partnered with OpenAI's o3 reasoning model to solve the one-dimensional frustrated Potts model -- a theoretical physics problem that had resisted solution for decades -- in a single day, revealing a phase diagram with applications in computing, electronics, and quantum devices.

3. Biology: GPT-5 Runs a Real Lab

GPT-5 autonomously ran tens of thousands of lab experiments autonomously in a cloud lab (a lab with robot arms that can be controlled remotely), and cut the cost of protein synthesis by 40%, significantly pushing the SOTA forward.

4. Coding: The Machine That Builds Itself

100% of code at Anthropic and OpenAI is now written by LLMs -- the machine is literally building the next version of itself.

5. Engineering: For Waterman

AECOM spent £300m (4% of their annual revenue) on acquiring Consigli, a specialist team of AI developers in engineering: PhDs, researchers, ML Scientists, AI engineers, and so on. They did this because Consigli was an existential risk to them.
